# WO-10871 — CNC-07 Tool-holder Imbalance: Vibration and Dimensional Drift

## 1. Symptom report

Quality technician L. Ferreira flagged rising dimensional deviation on Gear Assembly bore diameter (GA-4471) at 13:15. Six consecutive parts showed bore diameter 0.018–0.022 mm outside the +0.010 mm / -0.000 mm tolerance (oversized). Simultaneously, spindle vibration on CNC-07 was reading **3.9 mm/s RMS** (warning threshold: 3.6 mm/s). Cycle time stable at 43 s.

Machine stopped at 13:22 per SOP Tier 2 procedure. 18 parts held for quality inspection (serial GA-4471-2201 to GA-4471-2218).

---

## 2. Diagnostic sequence

### Step 1 — Tool and tool-holder inspection (13:30–13:55)

This step was completed first as per the CNC spindle inspection guide — vibration + dimensional drift together is the primary indicator for tool-holder investigation before bearing work.

- **Tool holder taper contact:** 87 % blued contact — within acceptance (≥ 85 %).
- **Balance class marking on holder:** G6.3 at 15,000 RPM. **This is out of specification.** LINE-02 requires G2.5 at 25,000 RPM for all holders used above 12,000 RPM. CNC-07 runs this job at 14,000 RPM.
- **Runout at 50 mm gauge length:** 0.0081 mm TIR. **Exceeds the 0.003 mm acceptance limit by 2.7×.**
- **Physical inspection:** No visible damage or chips. The imbalance is inherent to the holder specification — it was incorrectly issued from tooling store. Tooling store record shows this holder (serial T-07-284) was returned from CNC-04 overhaul 6 weeks ago and placed back on the rack without re-verification of balance class.

**Root cause confirmed: incorrect balance class tool holder causing centrifugal imbalance at operating spindle speed. This imbalance is generating both the spindle vibration and the dimensional deviation through micro-deflection during the boring pass.**

No spindle bearing inspection required — bearing spectrum was checked (Step 2 below) and confirmed normal.

### Step 2 — Spindle bearing check (14:00–14:20)

Spindle rotated by hand: smooth, no roughness. Accelerometer spectrum: broadband noise floor normal; no ball-pass frequency harmonics; 1× runout 0.0022 mm — within baseline. **Bearing cleared.**

---

## 3. Corrective action

1. Removed out-of-spec tool holder (T-07-284). Tagged for re-balancing or disposal — do not return to active rack without balance certification G2.5 or better.
2. Installed replacement balanced tool holder (SANDVIK-390.140-4032, balance class G2.5, from Tooling rack T-07, serial T-07-301). Verified runout: 0.0019 mm TIR — within acceptance.
3. Performed 5-minute spindle warm-up before production test.

---

## 4. Verification

Post-repair vibration at 14,000 RPM: **1.4 mm/s RMS** — normal operating band.  
Runout at 50 mm gauge length with new holder: **0.0019 mm TIR**.  
Test part bore diameter: 9.997 mm — within +0.010 / -0.000 tolerance.  
Dimensional result confirmed on 3 consecutive parts before releasing machine.

Quality inspection of 18 held parts: **all 18 rejected** — bore diameter 0.018–0.022 mm oversize. Reworked (bore can be re-bored to next oversize class for rework order GA-RW-0441).

---

## 5. Lessons learned and recommendations

**Root cause of the tooling issue:** The out-of-spec holder was placed back into active service after a CNC-04 overhaul without balance re-verification. The tooling store process does not currently require balance class re-check when a holder is returned from a machine strip-down.

**Action items raised:**
- **Action 1 (Tooling Supervisor):** Implement mandatory balance class verification for all holders returning from machine overhaul or strip-down before placing back on active rack. Add to Tooling Control Procedure TCP-008. Target: 2025-08-12.
- **Action 2 (TECH-334 + Tooling Supervisor):** Audit all holders currently on Tooling rack T-07 for correct balance class. Segregate any G6.3 holders — these are only acceptable for operations below 8,000 RPM. Complete by 2025-08-05.

**Key insight for future diagnostics:** When vibration and dimensional drift appear together and both rise simultaneously from the same point in time, investigate tool-holder balance and runout first. This failure mode does not present with gradual onset — it typically appears immediately after a tooling change or when an incorrectly-specified holder is loaded.

The absence of bearing symptoms (no roughness, normal spectrum) combined with the dimensional deviation pattern (consistent oversize, not random scatter) is a strong indicator of a deterministic mechanical cause — i.e., imbalance or runout rather than wear.
